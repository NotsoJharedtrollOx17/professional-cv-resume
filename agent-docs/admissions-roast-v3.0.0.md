# PhD Admissions Committee Roast - CV v3.0.0

## Scope and standard

This is an intentionally severe, CV-only review for:

- UC San Diego PhD in Data Science;
- UC San Diego PhD in Computer Science and Engineering; and
- UC Irvine - San Diego State University Joint PhD in Computational Science.

It does not evaluate the statement of purpose, transcript detail, recommendation letters, research statements, or application context that may materially strengthen the file. The standard used here is not "is this a good early-career resume?" It is "does this document make a selective research committee confident that the applicant can become an independent researcher?"

## Bottom-line committee verdict

The CV now presents a coherent candidate: a strong applied-AI engineer with a high undergraduate GPA, one supervised UC San Diego research experience, an undergraduate thesis, and a recent independent replication with public artifacts.

That is credible preparation. It is not yet a strong record of externally validated scholarship.

In a top-program pool, the likely committee label remains:

> Research-ready engineer with promising initiative; limited sustained and externally reviewed research evidence.

The CV is no longer an obvious reject on presentation or lack of direction. It is still vulnerable to rejection when compared with applicants who have longer laboratory appointments, stronger letters from active researchers, peer-reviewed or publicly reviewed outputs, and clearer evidence of individual intellectual ownership.

## Cons: reasons a selective committee may still reject

### 1. There is no externally validated scholarly output

The only manuscript is in preparation. There is no peer-reviewed paper, archival workshop paper, public preprint identifier, accepted abstract, or external review. A repository and internal seminar prove activity, not scholarly validation.

This is the largest weakness and cannot be repaired by wording.

### 2. The flagship research project is recent, independent, and a partial replication

The Emotion Vectors project began in April 2026. It has no listed advisor, collaborator, laboratory, venue, or external evaluator. The work demonstrates initiative and reproducibility, but its current status does not establish sustained research independence or a novel contribution.

Likely margin note: "Who has assessed whether this work is methodologically sound?"

### 3. The independent study's controls remain limited

The CV correctly avoids claiming a general affective geometry result. The underlying study still appears vulnerable on external affect validation, random-direction controls, layer selection, uncertainty estimates, prompt sensitivity, generated-corpus quality, and held-out validation. Those limitations should remain explicit in the manuscript and statement of purpose.

### 4. The ENLACE result is good but under-specified

The exact AUC comparison is valuable, but a research committee may still want:

- train, validation, and test counts;
- class balance and split construction;
- repeated-run variability or uncertainty;
- parameter, latency, or memory comparison with the GNN;
- the reason the simpler model matters scientifically or operationally; and
- a precise statement of Abraham's individual contribution relative to the coauthor and laboratory code.

Do not invent these details. Add them only when they can be verified.

### 5. The thesis cannot support causal language

The design is small, apparently non-randomized, and confounded. The revised wording correctly says that the assisted condition showed no advantage and that the study avoided a causal interpretation. A quantitatively trained reviewer may still inspect allocation, missing data, outcome construction, effect sizes, confidence intervals, and test selection.

### 6. Samsung still reports output volume more clearly than impact

"10+ prototypes" and "4 demonstrations" show throughput and communication, but not adoption, benchmark improvement, latency, memory, reliability, deployment, patent activity, or engineering decisions that changed a product. If confidentiality permits, one defensible impact or constraint metric would be worth more than another technology category.

### 7. Teaching scale is documented; learning impact is not

The third teaching bullet now adds a distinct pedagogical and program-improvement signal. It does not claim that students learned more or passed because no such evaluation is documented. If legitimate evidence exists - assessed improvement, materials created, workshop counts, repeat attendance interpreted carefully, or formal feedback - it could strengthen this entry.

Three bullets are not inherently better than two. Symmetry is not evidence.

### 8. The CV lacks conventional distinction signals

No honors, competitive scholarships, academic awards, class rank, selected advanced coursework, research grants, or professional research memberships appear. Their absence may simply reflect the record, but the committee cannot credit information that is not present.

### 9. The public identity remains a professionalism risk

The GitHub and website handle `NotsoJharedtrollOx17` may be memorable for the wrong reason. A reviewer who follows the links will also judge repository prose, documentation quality, commit history, and AI-assistance disclosures. The artifacts must look more rigorous than the handle looks informal.

### 10. The visual design is polished but slightly portfolio-like

The bright orange headings and saturated blue links are readable, but they are more expressive than a conventional academic CV. This is not a rejection issue. Content credibility matters far more, and no redesign is warranted unless the user wants a more conservative academic variant.

This is the most important entry and the largest credibility risk. The revised bullets now state the transfer question, the experimental scope, the analyses, the qualified findings, and the limitations. Do not promote the manuscript to `Preprint` until it is publicly posted as a stable manuscript with verifiable metadata.

The revised entry makes the design and non-causal conclusion visible. This is stronger than listing software or statistical-test names. Keep the mixed result; it is more credible than trying to make the intervention sound successful.

### ENLACE research

The revised entry explains why the AUC comparison matters: contrary to the team's initial expectation, the substantially simpler fully connected network performed within 0.0027 ROC AUC of the laboratory GNN baseline on the shared held-out data. Dataset and attribution details remain the next factual improvements.

### Industry

This section is strong professional evidence but secondary academic evidence. The revised final bullet frames the demonstrations as synthesis of prototype findings and integration constraints. Add product-impact metrics only when disclosure is permitted and the metric can be defended.

### Teaching and mentoring

The new third bullet is justified because it adds pedagogy and program-improvement evidence, not because other entries happen to have three bullets. It distinguishes student scale, teaching practice, and analysis/recommendations.

### Manuscript and presentations

The status labels are honest. The internal Samsung seminar is not external peer review. The Tizen talk demonstrates technical communication but is peripheral to the research narrative. It is acceptable while space remains; cut it before cutting stronger research evidence.

### Skills

The list is mostly demonstrated in the experience entries. SciPy was added because the thesis analysis supports it. Avoid adding fashionable labels that are not visible in a project.
